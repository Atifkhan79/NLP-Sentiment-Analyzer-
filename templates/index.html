What this project does

Real-time inference

One review is cleaned, vectorized, and classified in a single round trip — no batching delay for a quick check.

Bulk batch analysis

Paste dozens of reviews at once; a five-worker request pool classifies them concurrently and rolls up the split.

Pipeline transparency

Every prediction shows the exact lemmatized, stopword-stripped tokens the vectorizer actually saw — nothing is hidden.

Model benchmarking

Naive Bayes and Linear SVM are trained on the same split so their accuracy and per-class metrics can be compared directly.

Under the hood

Feature extraction

TF‑IDF vectorization

Every token is scored by how often it appears in a review versus how common it is across all 200k+ reviews — rare, distinctive words like “cracked” outweigh filler words like “the”.

Stopwords collapse toward zero — the vector is dominated by words that actually carry sentiment.

Classification

Linear SVM vs. Naive Bayes

The SVM finds the widest possible margin between classes in TF‑IDF space; the points on that margin — the support vectors — are the only ones that define the boundary. Hover the diagram to see them.

How it works

  1. 01

    The review is lowercased, stripped of punctuation, tokenized, and reduced to lemmas with stopwords removed.

  2. 02

    The cleaned text is converted into a TF‑IDF vector — a numeric fingerprint of which words matter and how rare they are.

  3. 03

    A linear SVM, trained on 200k+ labeled comments, classifies that vector as Negative, Neutral, or Positive.
